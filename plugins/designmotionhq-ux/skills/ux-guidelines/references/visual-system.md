# Visual system

Tokens, type, colour roles, spacing, radius, elevation and hierarchy. These rules are palette-independent: they define relationships (how many colours, which role gets the accent, how surfaces step), never specific brand colours. Always use the project's own palette and tokens.

Severity: **P0** unreadable or inaccessible, **P1** inconsistent or confusing, **P2** polish.

---

### VIS-1 · Nothing is typed raw (P1)
**Applies to.** all styling (always check).
**Rule.** Every colour, size, space, radius, shadow, weight, duration, easing and z-index comes from a named token on a fixed scale. Layer tokens: **primitives** (raw values; value names such as a numbered 100 to 900 shade ramp per hue are fine here) → **semantic** (`--color-bg`, `--color-surface`, `--color-text-muted`, `--color-accent`, `--color-danger`) → **component** (`--button-bg`, `--card-padding`). Components read only semantic or component tokens, never primitives, so one changed primitive or repointed alias cascades everywhere. Name by role, not value (`--color-accent`, `--space-md`, not `--blue-500`, `--spacing-16`); a rebrand repoints semantic tokens to new primitives rather than mutating a primitive into a value its name no longer describes. Themes, including dark mode, swap the semantic layer and are re-checked for contrast (VIS-7); components never change. A shared component ships with its variants, sizes and every state (STA-1, INT-10), not just the default.
**Detect.** Hex/rgb literals or px values inside components; components referencing primitive ramps (`var(--gray-200)`) directly; near-duplicate values (13px next to 12px, 15 or 17px next to 16px); value names (hue, px) on tokens used for meaning.
**Fix.** Snap stray values to the nearest scale step (13 → 12, 17 → 16, 22 → 24) and replace literals with tokens. If the project has no tokens, propose a minimal set before refactoring (see the "Missing design system" note in SKILL.md).

### VIS-2 · One spacing scale (P1)
**Applies to.** all spacing: padding, margin, gap, grid gutters.
**Rule.** One base unit (4 px or 8 px) and a short scale of selected steps (for example 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64 as `--space-1` … `--space-16`); every padding, margin and gap uses only these, with 2 px as the one documented exception for hairline or icon nudges. Random gaps (7, 11, 23) read as sloppy even when nobody can say why. A proportional scale (×1.5 or ×1.618: 8, 13, 21, 34, 55) is fine if it is the only scale; never mix its off-grid steps into a 4/8 px system, because one consistent scale matters more than which ratio it uses.
**Detect.** Many distinct spacing values; arbitrary Tailwind values (`p-[13px]`) for spacing; values not divisible by 4 in a 4 px system.

### VIS-3 · Spacing does the grouping (P1)
**Applies to.** every layout that groups items: toolbars, navigation and sidebar lists, forms, cards, page sections.
**Rule.** The gap inside a group is clearly smaller than the gap between groups, roughly 1:3 to 1:4 (for example 8 px between toolbar icons and 32 px between toolbar groups; 12 px between related fields and 40 px between form sections). Group toolbar, sidebar and nav items by function, and let the larger gap (plus a short group heading) draw the boundary before adding borders, boxes or dividers. A label sits closer to its own field than to the field above. Three kinds of white space: micro (padding inside elements), macro (between sections, often 40 px and more), active (deliberate emptiness around the one element that should pop, VIS-12). Cards and buttons that look cheap usually need more padding (about 24 px inside a card, 16 px between its elements) and more line-height in body text, not a redesign. On narrow screens shrink gaps by scale steps but keep the inside/between ratio.
**Detect.** Uniform gaps everywhere (an evenly spaced toolbar, a flat list of eight sidebar links); borders and dividers doing work spacing could do; label-to-input gap as large as the field-to-field gap; cramped padding (8 to 12 px on cards); body line-height near 1.1 to 1.2.
**Fix.** Give each visual group a semantic container as well (`fieldset` + `legend`, a `section` with a heading, `role="group"` inside `role="toolbar"`), so screen readers get the grouping that spacing shows.

### VIS-4 · Type: seven decisions made once (P1)
**Applies to.** all text styling.
**Rule.**
1. **Sizes**: about five steps from one base and one ratio. About 1.2 to 1.333 suits product UI (1.25: 13, 16, 20, 25, 31); 1.5 to 1.618 jumps fast (16, 26, 42, 68) and suits only marketing pages with few levels. Steps strictly increase with their names; set them in `rem` and `clamp()` the display step on small screens. Stop adding sizes.
2. **Inks**: three text roles (primary, secondary, muted) rank text within a line; size ranks between blocks.
3. **Weights**: two or three (for example 400 to read, 600 to scan, 700 to 800 for display). Keep small text at 400 or heavier, especially light on dark: a 300 caption is hard to read. Use only weights the font actually ships (or a variable axis), or the browser fakes bold.
4. **Leading moves against size**: body about 1.5, large headings about 1.1 to 1.2. Not one line-height for everything.
5. **Tracking**: tighten large headings slightly (about -0.02em above 24 px); open small uppercase labels (about +0.05em).
6. **Measure**: cap paragraphs at about 45 to 75 characters (`max-width: 66ch`), whatever the container width.
7. **Figures**: tabular numbers wherever digits align or update (DAT-1).
Define each step as one token bundling size, weight, line height and tracking; type keeps its own scale rather than borrowing spacing steps. Use one family (two at most).
**Detect.** More than about six font sizes; steps out of order (a "2xl" smaller than an "lg"); font sizes in px only; many weights; weights below 400 on small text; uniform line-height; full-width paragraphs; mixed families.

### VIS-5 · Colour roles: dominant, secondary, one accent (P1)
**Applies to.** every screen: navigation, buttons, links, metrics, badges, status pills.
**Rule.** Distribute colour by role, roughly 60/30/10: a dominant neutral for backgrounds, a secondary step of the same family for surfaces (cards, nav, containers), and one accent spent only on primary actions, links, the active state and the one key number. Status colours (danger, success, warning) are semantic and used only for their meaning. When every nav item or metric gets its own colour, nothing stands out; mute everything else and the primary pops. The ground affects how a colour reads, so check the accent on the surfaces it actually sits on.
**Detect.** Many unrelated hues on one screen; a different colour per nav item or metric; accent colour on decorative elements; coloured status pills everywhere; a primary button that blends into its card.

### VIS-6 · The danger colour is a budget (P1)
**Applies to.** uses of the danger or error colour.
**Rule.** The danger colour appears only on destructive actions and real errors. A red logout button or red decorative badges make the real delete look routine.
**Detect.** Danger tokens on non-destructive controls.

### VIS-7 · Contrast is a ratio (P0)
**Applies to.** every text and essential UI element (always check).
**Rule.** Contrast belongs to a text/background pair, never to a colour token alone: the same text colour can pass on one surface and vanish on the next. Body text at least 4.5:1 against its actual background; large text (24 px, or about 18.7 px bold) and essential UI parts (input borders, focus rings, icons that carry meaning) at least 3:1; aim for 7:1 (AAA) on critical text such as errors, prices and terms where the palette allows. Muted greys on nav links, card labels, subtitles, stat labels and placeholders are the usual failures, often only 1.5 to 2:1. Text dimmed by opacity is measured as rendered on its surface, gradient text at its weakest point. Check every pair in light and dark themes. Disabled controls are exempt, but their label stays legible.
**Detect.** Light grey text tokens; `opacity` below about 0.6 on text; text on images or gradients without a scrim; disabled-looking labels used for real content.
**Fix.** Change the lightness of the failing token, keeping its hue and the layout; a redesign is rarely needed.

### VIS-8 · Colour never carries meaning alone (P0)
**Applies to.** every status, state, series or group shown with colour: error and success messages, trends, chart series, chips, active nav items, selected icons (always check).
**Rule.** Every colour cue is paired with a second signal: an icon on error and success messages, an arrow plus a signed number on trends ("+14%"), patterns, textures or direct labels on chart series with shapes in the legend, a check or fill change on active chips, an underline or filled shape plus `aria-current` on the active nav item, a heading or position for colour-coded groups. Red against green is exactly the difference red-green colour-blind users (about 1 in 12 men) lose, so error and success, pass and fail, up and down never differ by hue alone. Before release, view key screens through a protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia simulator.
**Detect.** Status shown only by text or dot colour; red borders as the only error signal; charts distinguishable only by hue; groups distinguished only by tint.
**Fix.** For form errors add the icon, `aria-invalid="true"` and `aria-describedby` pointing at the message, so the meaning is not visual at all.

### VIS-9 · Dark mode is its own system (P1)
**Applies to.** products with a dark theme.
**Rule.** Not an inversion. Start from a near-black neutral (around 7% lightness), never pure black, with three or more surface tokens that get lighter as they rise (about +5 lightness points per step: base, surface, elevated). Elevation is shown by lightness because shadows barely show on dark; a darker, larger shadow may still support the top layers. Text is one light colour at a few opacities, never pure white: about 87 to 90% for headings and primary text, 60 to 70% for body and secondary, about 38% for disabled; every tier used for real content still meets VIS-7 on its surface. The accent keeps its hue but loses saturation (roughly 20%) and gains lightness so it does not vibrate or glow; then re-check the label on the accent fill and switch it to dark text, or darken the fill, if it drops below 4.5:1. Hairlines are white at a low alpha (about 6 to 12%) so they adapt to every surface; focus rings and input borders get their own dark values at 3:1. Photos are dimmed slightly; illustrations get dark variants. Text may need one weight step lighter. Implement as a semantic token swap (VIS-1), respecting `prefers-color-scheme` and any manual choice.
**Detect.** `filter: invert()`; `#000` backgrounds or `#fff` text; one surface colour for page, cards and popovers; the same saturated accent in both themes; a light label on a softened accent with no contrast check; fixed grey borders; shadows as the only elevation in dark.

### VIS-10 · Radius is a scale and a brand decision (P2)
**Applies to.** rounded elements (tooltips, badges, inputs, buttons, cards, modals, sheets, panels, avatars, focus rings), especially nested ones.
**Rule.** One scale (for example 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, plus a separate full/pill token) and radius grows with surface size: tooltips and badges 4, inputs and buttons 8, cards 12, modals 16, large panels and sheets 24; a small element is never rounder than the surface it sits on. Full radius only for one-line elements (pills, avatars, toggles). Nested corners stay concentric: **inner radius = outer radius − padding** (16 outer, 8 padding → 8 inner), also minus the border width when the outer has a visible border, never below zero; giving the inner element the outer radius makes it look bloated. A ring outside an element is outer = inner + gap. Edges that touch the screen get no corner (a bottom sheet is rounded only on top). Let the container clip images (`overflow: hidden`). Choose the range once for the brand: the small end reads serious and corporate, the large end friendly, pills playful; rounded controls feel safer to touch.
**Detect.** The same radius on a card and the element nested inside it; off-scale radii (6, 10, 20, 30); small elements rounder than their containers; full radius on multi-line cards.
**Fix.** `border-radius: max(0px, calc(var(--radius-outer) - var(--pad)));` on nested surfaces.

### VIS-11 · Elevation encodes hierarchy (P1)
**Applies to.** shadows, glows and elevated layers: menus, popovers, modals, dragged items, featured cards.
**Rule.** Elevation tells the user what sits above what, so spend it only where something floats or matters most, from a small token scale (levels 0 to 4). Build each level from layers, never one blur: a tight, darker contact shadow at the edge (about 1 to 2 px offset, 2 to 3 px blur), a softer mid shadow (about 8 px offset, 12 to 24 px blur) and optionally a wide ambient one (about 32 to 40 px blur), each larger layer fainter. Page panels and rows get none; separate them with surface value steps and hairline borders. A shadow on every card means nothing floats: the strongest level goes to the one element that matters most (the recommended plan, the open modal), which also says so in text ("Recommended", `aria-modal`), since elevation is not announced. A soft glow tinted from the accent token (low alpha, about 15 to 35% on dark, far fainter on light) is an optional premium touch on that one element only. To animate elevation, fade a pseudo-element that holds the larger shadow instead of transitioning `box-shadow`.
**Detect.** The same heavy drop shadow on every panel; a single hard `box-shadow: 0 0 10px #000`; shadows separating table rows or sidebars; glows on several elements; a glow colour typed in rather than derived from the accent token; `transition: box-shadow` on hover.
**Example.**
```css
--shadow-float: 0 1px 3px rgb(0 0 0 / .12), 0 8px 24px rgb(0 0 0 / .12);
--shadow-glow: 0 0 60px color-mix(in srgb, var(--color-accent) 15%, transparent);
```

### VIS-12 · One focal point per view (P1)
**Applies to.** every screen; pricing tables, plan pickers, hero sections, nav bars and forms with a primary action.
**Rule.** Stack five levers on one entry point: size (the primary heading about twice body size; +20% reads as the same level), colour (one accent), contrast (bold against regular, filled against outlined, strong against muted text), space (about double the padding of its neighbours, one or two scale steps up) and weight. Primary action filled, secondary outline or text link, one filled primary per view: when a form's primary button is on screen, demote the nav CTA. When one option must stand out (a pricing tier, a CTA), start from a calm uniform baseline and change that one item on several channels at once (scale, accent fill, elevation, a truthful badge such as "Most popular", a specific label like "Get Pro" instead of the shared "Choose plan"), never colour alone. Quiet the alternatives with weight, fill and elevation, not by pushing their text below 4.5:1; users still compare them. Heading levels (`h1` to `h3`) follow the visual order.
**Detect.** Several equally loud buttons side by side; two accent-filled CTAs in one view; headings barely larger than body; everything bold; identical pricing cards with no featured variant; dimmed plan text that fails contrast.
**Fix.** Express emphasis as a component variant (`primary` / `secondary` / `ghost`; a `featured` card prop that changes elevation, border and badge) so only one primary exists per view. On mobile, stack the featured plan first with its badge rather than scaling it into overflow.

### VIS-13 · Layout sits on a grid, then breaks it on purpose (P2)
**Applies to.** page and section layouts, dashboards, app shells with sidebars.
**Rule.** A 12-column grid (or the project's grid) whose spans are ratios that state hierarchy: 4:8 for a narrow sidebar and wide content, 3:9 for a minimal sidebar and dominant content, 6:6 only for genuinely equal content; an unequal split (about 62/38) puts primary content on the larger side. At breakpoints reconfigure the grid instead of abandoning it (for example 12 columns at about 1280 px, 6 at 768, 4 at 480, 1 at 320) and re-declare spans for each. The gutter is a design choice on the spacing scale: about 8 px dense and technical, 24 px balanced, 40 px editorial, usually smaller on mobile. Align to shared edges. Break the grid deliberately (a full-bleed hero, a pull quote into the margin) only once the order exists. Visual reordering never changes reading or focus order, the single column works at 320 CSS px without horizontal scroll, and when columns narrow, decide where content wraps rather than dropping it.
**Detect.** Elements that almost align; ad-hoc widths; centred everything; `repeat(12, 1fr)` without `minmax(0, 1fr)` (long content blows out a column); `order` or grid placement that differs from DOM order; full-bleed via `width: 100vw` (adds horizontal scroll); horizontal scroll at 320 px.
**Fix.** `grid-template-columns: repeat(12, minmax(0, 1fr)); column-gap: var(--gutter);`, with named lines (`[full-start] … [content-start] … [content-end] … [full-end]`) and `grid-column: full` for bleeds.

### VIS-14 · Gestalt does free work (P2)
**Applies to.** layouts that group or order items: settings lists, navigation, toolbars, icon bars, card grids, dialogs over a page.
**Rule.** Proximity comes first (VIS-3). Similarity: things that belong together share one property, and changing that one property (not several) splits groups. Continuity: items on one axis read as a sequence, so align nav and lists instead of scattering them. Common region: a card or bordered section with a heading groups related controls more strongly than spacing, especially when they sit far apart (one card per settings group). Figure-ground: a dimmed scrim, optionally blurred, behind a dialog pushes it forward; dimming alone is enough on low-end devices. Closure: implied edges from spacing, alignment or partial outlines replace drawn boxes and dividers, but a barely visible implied border is never the only edge of a control (VIS-7). Back colour grouping with position, headings or shape (VIS-8).
**Detect.** Dialogs over an undimmed page; related settings scattered without a container; misaligned or scattered nav items; groups told apart only by colour; boxes and dividers around everything.

### VIS-15 · Icons are one set (P2)
**Applies to.** icons and icon buttons in toolbars, menus, nav bars and lists.
**Rule.** One family (one established library rather than several), one stroke weight across the set (2 px at 24 px is the common default; mixed weights look like four libraries), and one style rule: outline by default, filled only for the active or selected state, never a random mix. Draw on a 24 px grid (16 px for dense UI) with nodes on whole pixels, and render at the design size or integer steps (16, 20, 24, 32) so strokes stay crisp; if icons scale, keep the effective stroke equal across a row (`vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke` or the library's absolute-stroke option). Every icon sits in the same fixed box (the glyph varies, the container does not), which is also its hit area and is padded to the target sizes in INT-11. Round and pointed shapes are drawn larger than square ones to look the same size (VIS-16). Icons sit centred on the text line. A fill-for-active change also sets `aria-pressed` or `aria-current`; icon-only buttons get an accessible name, decorative icons `aria-hidden="true"`, meaningful icons 3:1 contrast.
**Detect.** Mixed icon libraries; mixed `stroke-width`; filled and outlined icons mixed without meaning; icons of different sizes in one toolbar; SVGs at fractional sizes or positions (an even icon centred in an odd box); icon buttons without `aria-label`; icons misaligned with adjacent text.

### VIS-16 · Optical corrections beat maths (P2)
**Applies to.** play buttons, icon and shape pairs, text in buttons and badges.
**Rule.** When the correct value looks wrong, adjust for the eye: shift a play triangle right (about 8% of its box), draw circles about 5 to 8% larger than squares beside them (triangles and other pointed shapes need more; judge at the rendered size), keep nested corners concentric, centre text on its cap height (labels in buttons often sit visually low), and reduce weight one step on light-on-dark text.

### VIS-17 · Gradients and glass are seasoning (P2)
**Applies to.** gradients (buttons, hero and card backgrounds, gradient text) and translucent "glass" surfaces.
**Rule.** Gradients stay between neighbouring hues (at most about 60° of hue travel; complementary ends turn muddy in the middle), derive their stops from the accent token, move lightness in one direction, and interpolate in a perceptual space (`in oklch`) to avoid a grey dead zone. Linear ramps on surfaces and buttons read best on a diagonal that matches the product's implied light source (top-left to bottom-right, `135deg`, is the usual pick); plain vertical or horizontal ramps look like default fills. Large surfaces get a few soft radial glows (2 to 4 points) as ambiance behind content rather than a wash over it. Gradient text only on one large headline, checked for contrast at its weakest point, with a solid `color` fallback and a forced-colours fallback; body text stays solid and never sits on the transition zone. Add subtle grain (about 2 to 3% noise) if banding shows. Glass (translucent fill, `backdrop-filter: blur`, light hairline, soft shadow) only works over a busy backdrop and must keep text contrast.
**Detect.** Gradient text in body copy; `background-clip: text` with no solid fallback; gradients across opposite or many hues; sRGB ramps between distant hues; glass panels over flat colour; translucent panels that fail contrast.

### VIS-18 · Layering without z-index wars (P1)
**Applies to.** overlapping layers: dropdowns, menus, popovers, tooltips, modals, toasts, sticky headers.
**Rule.** `z-index` only orders positioned elements (relative, absolute, fixed, sticky) and flex or grid items, and only against siblings in the same stacking context: a child at 9999 inside a parent context at 1 still sits below a sibling context at 2. Contexts come not only from position plus z-index but also from `opacity` below 1, `transform`, `filter`, `backdrop-filter`, `will-change`, `contain` and fixed or sticky position, so an animated ancestor is the usual reason a high number "does nothing". Give reusable components their own context with `isolation: isolate` (no position or layout change) and keep a small named scale (base, dropdown, sticky, overlay, modal, toast). Render menus, popovers and dialogs outside clipping and stacking ancestors, through a portal or the top layer (`<dialog>` with `showModal()`, the `popover` attribute); `isolation` does not escape `overflow: hidden`. Debug by finding the ancestor that creates the context (computed styles, a 3D z-index view), not by escalating numbers.
**Detect.** Escalating values (999, 9999, 99999); `z-index` on a static element that is not a flex or grid item; component-internal z-indexes without `isolation: isolate`; overlays rendered inside a transformed or `overflow: hidden` ancestor.

### VIS-19 · Depth tricks stay subtle (P2)
**Applies to.** 3D tilt and `translateZ` effects, hover lift on cards and tiles, parallax layers.
**Rule.** Transform-based depth supports shadows and surface steps (VIS-11, VIS-9); it never replaces them. `translateZ` and `rotateX` only read inside a perspective (about 800 to 1200 px on the container, or `perspective()` in the transform); without one only the scale does anything. Keep it small: a lift of a few pixels, scale about 1.02 to 1.03, tilt about 2°, on hover or press rather than at rest (a tilted card can blur its text), and reinforce the lift with a stronger shadow or border. Parallax speeds are relative: the foreground moves with the scroll and background layers at a fraction of it, never faster. Scope hover depth to `@media (hover: hover)`, give `:focus-visible` the same emphasis, and remove tilt and parallax under reduced motion (MOT-13); hover timing is MOT-9, scroll binding MOT-12.
**Detect.** `translateZ` with no `perspective` on an ancestor; permanent `rotateX`/`rotateY` on text-bearing cards; hover scale above about 1.05; layers moved faster than the scroll; parallax with no reduced-motion handling.

### VIS-20 · Cards: padding, hierarchy, an edge (P2)
**Applies to.** cards and tiles: content, product and dashboard cards.
**Rule.** A card looks finished when four things are decided. Padding: generous and even (about 24 px on all sides, 16 px between image, tag, title, body and meta), never content pressed to the edge. Hierarchy: the title clearly leads (weight 600 and larger, about 18 px against 14 px), the description is regular weight in the muted text token, which still passes 4.5:1 on the card (VIS-7). Edge: two stacked shadows (VIS-11), and on dark surfaces a 1 px hairline at about 10 to 12% of the text colour, since shadows barely show there; radius from the scale (VIS-10). Hover: only if the whole card is clickable, a lift with a stronger shadow and border, mirrored on `:focus-visible` (MOT-9). A clickable card is one link or button target, not several nested ones.
**Detect.** Card padding under about 16 px; title and description in the same size and weight; flat cards on a dark page with neither hairline nor surface step; hover lift on a card that does nothing; several nested interactive elements inside a clickable card.
