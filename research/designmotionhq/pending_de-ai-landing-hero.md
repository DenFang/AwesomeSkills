# De-AI landing hero: five tells, five fixes

**Caption:** not captured yet
**Hook:** A typical generated landing page is recognisable from five tells; each has a concrete fix.

## Main rule
Replace every decoration in the hero with something concrete about the product: an outcome headline plus an audience subtitle, a neutral background with the only accent on the product and one primary button, one primary CTA plus a text link, one attributed quote with a measurable result, and a real product screenshot with annotations instead of an icon-feature row.

## Before / after
- **Tell 1, headline:** Bad: gradient-filled headline of adjectives ("Supercharge your workflow with AI") and a subtitle of filler ("all-in-one", "seamlessly"); tagged "0 words about the product". Good: plain-text headline naming the outcome ("Close the month in one afternoon.") with a subtitle naming the category and the audience ("Bookkeeping for small teams, without the spreadsheet.", "small teams" tagged "for whom"); chip "Outcome first · plain text". Why: a visitor decides in seconds whether the page is for them; adjectives carry no information, an outcome plus audience answers "what is it and is it for me". Gradient text also lowers legibility.
- **Tell 2, the blob:** Bad: a large blurred two-hue gradient smear behind the hero, added only to supply energy. Good: neutral background; the hero gains a real product dashboard panel (overview card with revenue, unreconciled count, days to close, chart, transaction rows) tagged "product", and accent colour is used on a single primary button tagged "one button". Chip "1 accent · 0 blobs". Why: colour is an attention signal; spent on a background it points at nothing, spent on the product and the CTA it directs the eye to what matters.
- **Tell 3, two buttons:** Bad: "Get started" and "Learn more" both 200 × 64, both filled, side by side; click-weight meter 50 / 50. Good: one filled primary ("Start free") and the second action as a text link with an arrow ("See how it works →"); meter 90 / 10. Why: two equal buttons force a choice and split attention; visual weight should match the intended next step, while the secondary path stays available.
- **Tell 4, the proof:** Bad: "Trusted by 10,000+ users" above five grey logos nobody recognises; the number is tagged "unverifiable" and each logo is flagged. Good: one testimonial card: the quote with a concrete before/after result, the person's name and role ("Head of Finance" at a named company), avatar initials; annotations "a name", "a role", "a result with a unit". Why: specific, attributable claims are believable; round unverifiable numbers and anonymous logos read as template filler.
- **Tell 5, feature cards:** Bad: three cards, icon in a tinted circle, one-word title (Fast / Secure / Easy) with generic one-liners and "Learn more"; tagged "same row, every generated page". Good: one product screenshot (an overview dashboard with metrics, chart and recent transactions) with three callouts pointing at real UI ("Bank sync, every morning", "Export to your accountant in one click", "Categories learned from your history"). Chip "Show the product · not the adjectives". Why: showing a real interface proves the feature exists and lets the visitor imagine using it; generic icon cards claim qualities without evidence.

## Do
- Write the headline as a concrete outcome and put the audience in the subtitle.
- Use plain (solid) text for headlines.
- Keep the background neutral; reserve the single accent colour for the primary CTA and the product visual.
- Show one primary button; render the secondary action as a text link with an arrow.
- Use one testimonial with name, role, company and a result with a unit (time, money, percentage).
- Replace the feature row with a real screenshot plus about three annotations pointing at specific UI elements.

## Don't
- Don't use adjective headlines (supercharge, seamless, powerful, all-in-one).
- Don't put a blurred gradient blob behind the hero for "energy".
- Don't place two identically sized, identically filled buttons side by side.
- Don't use unverifiable user counts with unrecognisable logos as social proof.
- Don't ship the three icon-in-a-circle cards with one-word labels.

## Voice-over summary
- Announces five tells of an AI-made landing page and five fixes.
- Headline: a generated hero sells adjectives because it has no product to name; write plain text that says what it does and for whom.
- Blob: the gradient smear exists because the page needs energy from somewhere; use a neutral background and put the only colour on the product and one button.
- Buttons: two same-size buttons split the click; keep one primary and turn the second into a text link with an arrow.
- Proof: "a number nobody can check, logos nobody recognizes"; use one quote with a name, a role and a result with a unit.
- Features: replace the generic three-card row with one screenshot and three annotations on real UI. Ends with a plugin advertisement.

## Notes for the skill
- Colour role rule: one accent, used for the primary CTA and to highlight the product visual; background and surfaces neutral. Check accent button text contrast at least 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3) and its boundary against the background at least 3:1 (1.4.11).
- Gradient-filled text (`background-clip: text`) has no single contrast value and degrades in forced-colours / high-contrast modes; prefer solid text colour for headlines.
- Text-link secondary CTA: keep it a real `<a>` (navigation) or `<button>` (action) with a visible focus ring, a hit area of at least 24x24 CSS px (WCAG 2.5.8), and ideally underline or arrow so it is recognisable as interactive without colour alone (WCAG 1.4.1). The arrow glyph should be `aria-hidden="true"`.
- The 50/50 to 90/10 "click weight" meter is an illustrative visual-weight metaphor, not a measured click-through rate; do not cite it as data.
- Testimonials must be real and attributable; fabricated quotes or metrics are deceptive. If no real quote exists, show the product instead of inventing proof.
- Product screenshots: give meaningful `alt` text describing what the screenshot shows; annotations should be real text (not baked into the image) so they are readable by screen readers and scale with zoom; on narrow screens, move callouts below the image as a numbered list rather than overlapping it.
- Headline pattern: outcome (verb + measurable result) in the H1, category + audience in the subtitle; one H1 per page.
