# Tooltip design: delay, arrow, edge flip, dismissal, tight copy

**Caption:** Tooltip can be a nightmare for UX if badly implemented.
**Hook:** Most tooltips are annoying; five rules separate a cheap one (instant, wordy, covering its trigger) from a premium one (short label, arrow, appears on intent).

## Main rule
Show a hover tooltip only after about 300 ms of intent, point it at its trigger with an arrow, flip it to the opposite side when it would leave the viewport, close it on mouse leave, Escape, blur and outside tap, and keep it to one sentence within about 300 px width.

## Numbers and values
| Value | Applies to | Where shown |

## Before / after
- **Cheap vs premium (hook).** Cheap: appears instantly, a multi-line paragraph in a loud box that covers the trigger icon. Premium: a two-word label ("Get help") above the icon with an arrow, shown after a pause.
- **0 ms vs 300 ms delay.** Bad: tooltip pops every time the cursor crosses the icon on its way somewhere else. Good: shows only if the pointer rests about 300 ms ("intent confirmed"). Why: filters accidental passes, reduces visual noise and flicker.
- **No arrow vs arrow.** Bad: a label floating above a row of four icons (profile, notifications, settings, privacy) leaves users asking which icon it describes. Good: an arrow/tail points at the hovered icon and the tooltip centres on it. Why: explicit spatial link removes ambiguity.
- **Clipped vs flipped.** Bad: trigger near the right edge, tooltip renders off-screen and is cut off. Good: detect viewport bounds and place it on the side that fits ("Flipped in bounds"). Why: a clipped tooltip is worse than none.
- **Sticky vs dismissible.** Good: closes on mouse leave, Escape, focus out and tap outside. Why: a tooltip that stays open covers content and traps the user.
- **Paragraph vs one sentence.** Bad: 600 px wide, four lines explaining notification settings and pointing to docs. Good: 300 px max, "Controls notification defaults." Why: a tooltip is a hint glanced at in passing, longer help belongs in inline text, a popover or docs.

## Do
- Delay hover reveal by about 300 ms.
- Add an arrow anchored to the trigger centre.
- Use collision detection to flip/shift within the viewport.
- Close on pointer leave, Escape, blur and outside tap.
- Cap width near 300 px and copy at one sentence.

## Don't
- Don't show tooltips instantly on every graze.
- Don't let the tooltip cover its own trigger.
- Don't let it clip off-screen.
- Don't put documentation-length text (or links/actions) in a tooltip.

## Voice-over summary
- Wait 300 ms before showing; instant tooltips fire whenever a cursor passes, which is noise.
- Point with an arrow; without the tail users cannot tell what is being explained.
- Flip near viewport edges: "worse than no tooltip at all" when cut off; detect the edge and flip.
- Dismiss on mouse leave, Escape, focus out and tap outside; a sticky tooltip traps the user.
- Maximum 300 px wide and one sentence; a tooltip is a hint, not documentation.

## Notes for the skill
- WCAG 1.4.13 (Content on Hover or Focus) requires the tooltip to be dismissible without moving pointer/focus (Escape), hoverable (the pointer can move from trigger onto the tooltip without it vanishing) and persistent until dismissed. "Close on mouse leave" must therefore cover leaving both trigger and tooltip, usually with a short close grace delay (around 100 ms) so the pointer can cross the gap.
- Show on keyboard focus as well as hover (WAI-ARIA APG tooltip pattern): `role="tooltip"` on the bubble, `aria-describedby` on the trigger; the trigger keeps focus. Tooltips must not contain interactive content; use a popover/toggletip for that.
- Touch: hover does not exist, so either show on long-press or replace with a toggletip opened by tap (a small "i" button). Never hide essential information only in a tooltip; icon-only buttons still need an accessible name (`aria-label`).
- Delay tuning: once one tooltip is open, adjacent triggers can show immediately (skip-delay window) so scanning a toolbar is fast. Libraries default longer delays (e.g. Radix ~700 ms); 300 ms is a reasonable product choice, not a standard.
- Positioning: use a collision-aware positioner (Floating UI `flip()` + `shift()` + `arrow()`, or CSS anchor positioning with `position-try-fallbacks`) and keep the arrow aligned to the trigger after shifting.
- Motion: fade/scale-in roughly 100-150 ms with a small offset; under `prefers-reduced-motion` use opacity only. The reel does not give animation durations.
