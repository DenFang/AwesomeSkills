# Shadows: one light source, two layers, steady opacity

**Caption:** Uniform, one-size shadows give away a generated UI; give the interface a single light source, scale distance and softness with height, stack a contact and an ambient layer, and trim the ambient halo with negative spread.
**Hook:** The cover shows three cards whose shadows each point to a different sun, i.e. the shadows disagree about where the light is.

## Main rule
All shadows in an interface come from one implied light source, so they fall in the same direction. Elevation is expressed by moving the shadow farther and making it softer while keeping its opacity roughly constant, built from two stacked layers: a tight contact shadow and a soft ambient shadow whose negative spread removes the side halo.

## Before / after
- **Many suns vs one sun (video slide).** Bad: three cards at different positions, each with a sun in a different corner (top-left, top-right, bottom-right), so the hatched shadow shapes are offset in different directions. Good: one sun above the group, and every card's shadow drops straight down by the same amount. Why: the eye reads shadow direction as a cue for light and depth; conflicting directions make the layout look composited from unrelated parts, a consistent direction makes the cards read as objects in one space.
- **Higher element: darker shadow vs farther/softer shadow (caption only).** Bad: raising an element by making the shadow darker. Good: larger offset and blur at the same opacity. Why: in the real world a higher object casts a more diffuse, displaced shadow, not a blacker one; darker shadows look dirty, especially on light surfaces.
- **One blurry layer vs two layers (caption only).** Bad: a single shadow that is either crisp (flat, sticker-like) or soft (floating with no grounding). Good: a tight contact layer anchors the edge, a soft ambient layer gives lift. The negative spread on the soft layer keeps it from bleeding out to the sides as a halo.

## Do
- Define one light direction for the product (usually from above, so shadows offset downward on the y axis with x at or near zero) and use it for every elevation level.
- Build each elevation token from two shadows: small offset and small blur for contact, larger offset and blur with negative spread for ambient.
- Increase offset and blur with elevation; keep the alpha of each layer roughly the same across levels.
- Keep shadows as tokens (e.g. `--shadow-1` ... `--shadow-4`) so components never hand-pick values.

## Don't
- Don't mix shadows with different x offsets or directions on one screen.
- Don't use the same single shadow for every elevation (card, dropdown, modal all identical).
- Don't signal elevation by darkening the shadow alone.
- Don't let the ambient layer spread past the element sides as a glow-like halo.

## Notes for the skill
- Example token shape (values are illustrative, not from the post): `box-shadow: 0 1px 2px var(--shadow-contact), 0 8px 24px -8px var(--shadow-ambient);` for a raised card; for a higher layer keep the same alphas and grow to e.g. `0 2px 4px ..., 0 24px 48px -12px ...`. The negative spread roughly tracks a third to a half of the blur so the halo disappears at the sides.
- Use one shadow colour token with alpha (often a dark tint of the background hue rather than pure black) so shadows stay consistent across themes.
- Dark themes: shadows are barely visible on dark surfaces; express elevation mainly with slightly lighter surface tones or a subtle border, and keep the shadow as a secondary cue.
- Accessibility: shadows are decorative for cards, but if a shadow is the only thing that shows a control's boundary (e.g. a button or input), the boundary needs 3:1 contrast against adjacent colours (WCAG 1.4.11). Add a border or fill if the shadow alone is too faint, and check forced-colors mode, where box shadows are removed.
- Animate elevation changes (hover lift) by transitioning between two shadow tokens, or via an opacity transition on a pseudo-element for performance; respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- The claim that uniform shadows are a "tell" of generated UI is an opinion; the testable rules are the four in the caption.
